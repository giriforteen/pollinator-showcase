Segment One

The Fire

A forest fire doesn't just burn trees. It changes soil chemistry, water flow, animal migration, seed timing. Everything organized around that ecosystem—gone. You're not mourning a person. You're mourning an ecosystem. And your nervous system knows the difference.

Segment Two

The Silence

Right after fire, there's silence. Birds gone. Insects quiet. Just... still. Ecologists call this "shock phase." The system processing disruption. Your numbness isn't broken. Your silence isn't failure. It's recalibration.

Segment Three

Pioneer Species

First recovery signs aren't dramatic. Tiny plants. Lichens, mosses, fireweed. Small, unimpressive, essential. They stabilize soil. Fix nitrogen. Create conditions for what's next. Your "got out of bed" is fireweed. Your "texted a friend" is lichen. Pioneer species don't apologize for being small.

You're missing the ecosystem it maintained. The routines, identity, future plans. All structured around that keystone.

New Forest

Post-fire forest is never identical to pre-fire. Different species establish. New patterns emerge. Not better or worse. Different. The forest doesn't mourn its old composition. You won't be who you were before. You'll be new forest. Different ecosystem. Still whole. And that's not failure. That's ecology.
